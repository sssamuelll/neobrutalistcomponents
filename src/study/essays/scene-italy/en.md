## The Torre Velasca

In Milan, the Società Generale Immobiliare commissioned the Torre Velasca from BBPR, the firm Gian Luigi Banfi, Lodovico Barbiano di Belgiojoso, Enrico Peressutti and Ernesto Nathan Rogers had founded in 1932 [1]. Interni dates its construction to 1956–1958, on a lot cleared by wartime bombing [2]. The tower is 106 metres high, with a reinforced-concrete structure designed by Arturo Danusso, and holds offices and flats [1]. Reyner Banham attacked it in the Architectural Review in April 1959, the year the Italian delegation presented it at the CIAM congress in Otterlo [1]. For years the public counted it among the ugliest buildings in the world; in 2012 the Lombardy heritage authority declared it a monument [1][2]. None of these sources calls it brutalist.

## Olivetti and the Elea 9003

The Elea 9003, presented in 1959, was the first commercial electronic computer designed and built in Italy, made in a small workshop in Pisa by a team led by the engineer Mario Tchou [3][4]. Adriano Olivetti put the architect Ettore Sottsass in charge of its design; with Andries Van Onck, Sottsass worked on how people would operate and maintain it [3]. The machine sat in modular cabinets with coloured strips that named what each one held, and Sottsass set the racks at 150 centimetres so that engineers on either side could see each other over the noise [3]. It won the Compasso d’Oro in 1959 [5]. Sottsass worked with Olivetti from 1960 to 1980, and his Valentine typewriter of 1969 was bright red, in ABS plastic [5]; the Centre Pompidou showed those twenty years in 2003 [6].

## Superstudio and the grid

Adolfo Natalini and Cristiano Toraldo di Francia founded Superstudio in Florence in 1966, and the group broke up in 1977 [8]. Its first show, Superarchitettura, in Pistoia with Archizoom Associati, had brightly coloured furniture, pop graphics and ironic slogans [9]. The Continuous Monument of 1969, mostly photocollages, imagined a gridded structure wrapping the world across its borders [8]; Domus, which published it in December 1969, ties it to Land Art [7]. The grid later reached furniture, in the Quaderna series [9].

## Memphis

Memphis was formally set up on 11 December 1980 at Sottsass’s house in Milan, with Michele De Lucchi, Aldo Cibic, Matteo Thun, Marco Zanini and Martine Bedin among others [10]. Sottsass and De Lucchi came from the radical anti-design of the 1970s, Studio Alchimia among it [10]. The first collection opened on 18 September 1981 at the Arc ’74 gallery, during the Milan furniture fair, and used plain materials such as plastic laminate and terrazzo [10]. Its Carlton is in museums in Paris and Stockholm, which describe it as a five-level domestic totem and as closer to sculpture than to a bookcase [11][12].
